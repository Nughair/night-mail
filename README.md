Night Mail — pilot a paper plane over a moonlit city and slip letters through windows that glow gold. A twelve-route campaign of rising quotas, wind and rooftop density — Harbour Row, Old Town, the fog-bound Canal District, the Mill District, the Festival Quarter under its fireworks, the Heights, the Storm Quarter, the Lighthouse Coast, the Market District, the Rooftop Gardens, the Narrows — and finally the Storm Warden, a boss whose delivery lantern swings wider and faster with every letter it loses, whose gusts grow as the fight goes on, and who answers each delivery with a roar of wind. Each late district flies differently: fog banks hide the windows, rain bands shove the plane, the lighthouse beam presses down on whatever it catches, warm thermals over the market lift the plane over the stalls, the gardens' doves scatter it and break its combo, and the Narrows run under a low cloud deck with no climbing out. Some letters are special — express post pays double against a nine-second clock, and a fragile letter is voided by a single rooftop clip. After every route, draft one of three upgrades (a bigger mailbag, a tailwind charm, reinforced wings that survive one rooftop clip, and more) and carry it for the rest of the campaign. Every route is scored for a medal — bronze, silver, gold. Your progress is saved as you fly: medals, best scores, unlocked start nights, every upgrade ever collected into the postbag, and the campaign itself — leave mid-campaign and the title screen offers to continue exactly where the route began, and the campaign map lets you start a fresh run from any night you have unlocked. Beat the Storm Warden and the Night Owl opens, an endless night that loops the districts with rising quotas. Chain deliveries into combos, skim rooftops for near-miss bonuses, and outlast the Warden to win.
